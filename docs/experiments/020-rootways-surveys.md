# Experiment 020 — Rootways surveys and Dawn seal

Depth 50 unlocks a 750-Gold Cartography desk. Survey orders send the assigned party into its highest conquered non-Gatekeeper Rootways room. Victory gives normal Gold/XP and a blueprint discovery roll: 20% plus 10% per entering Ranger, capped at 50%. Survey does not advance the frontier. Discovery or defeat stops automatic surveys. Recall grants nothing. This is one authored discovery, not an expanding procedural exploration system.

The Dawn seal blueprint permits a separate 1,200-Gold workshop commission, then free equipment changes between rooms. First ability casts at 1 second, later casts use ordinary cooldown; basic attacks gain 10%. Its one slot competes with Soldier's seal (+35% basics, +2s cooldown) and Ritual focus (+35% ability strength, -20% basics). Opening mending can be wasted on a healthy party and shifts subsequent healing later. Ward and bleed benefit from an earlier cast without gaining recurring ritual strength.

`node tools/survey-study.js` records 14,400 encounters: three four-member compositions, five depths (51,55,65,71,75), three levels (12,20,28), four changed slots, four equipment choices and twenty paired seeds. Origins, techniques, levels and combat seeds stay fixed within comparisons; unchanged Rangers use steel, unchanged support uses ritual. Every terminal save validates. Raw final output: `020-survey-results.csv`.

An initial Dawn with -15% basics improved no win-rate comparison against the slot's usual steel/ritual choice and worsened thirteen. Reduced damage taken in already successful fights had limited value because recovery is free. Revised basics to +10% and repeated the same matrix. Final Dawn improves two win-rate comparisons and worsens eleven; most outcomes tie. In Guardian/Ranger/Cleric/Warden at level 20, changing the Ranger from steel to Dawn wins 20/20 at depth 65 versus 19/20 (35.9s versus 39.8s mean), and 7/20 at depth 75 versus 6/20 (44.0s versus 47.9s). Changing the Cleric at depth 65 wins 5/20 versus ritual's 19/20. These small paired cohorts support a situational choice, not a statistically established general advantage. Cost/value remains a playtesting question.

Sixty actual one-hour mixed-investment journey states test automatic surveying for four hours, comparing every live tick against bulk advance and validating final saves. All discover the blueprint, averaging 3.1 attempts with a maximum of 14 and longest elapsed search of 196s. All have one Ranger, so the advertised chance is 30%. Study reserves at least 750 Gold before purchase; it evaluates mechanical availability and continuation, not whether every journey can immediately afford the desk. The original journey policy makes purchases/recovery choices continuously, so these are not untouched idle opening sessions.

157 tests pass. New tests cover route bounds/cycles, class scouting odds, exact gates/costs, rewards, seeded discovery, independent recruitment RNG, entering purpose despite mid-combat order changes, failure/recall stops, equipment timing for all classes, saved plans/report snapshots, exact offline parity, legacy migration and invalid fields. Earlier in-flight purposes and report purposes remain unrecorded instead of inferred from current orders.

Browser Guild at frontier 50 purchased the desk, surveyed depth 49, discovered the blueprint and stopped. Commissioned Dawn and equipped Ilyra: inspection showed first Barbed arrow at 1s, then every 5s. A subsequent ordinary depth-49 victory recorded Dawn with 659 damage over 7 seconds; different seeds and entering levels prevent treating this as a controlled comparison with prior 8-second steel fights. Blueprint, commissioned equipment and report survived reload; no console warnings/errors. Existing saved plans retain their previous equipment.

Next: study actual Gold opportunity costs and wider techniques/near-frontier levels before adding more survey rewards. The single route adds Delve-to-Guild discovery with existing combat, Gold and equipment; it does not resolve repeated-region content or prove enjoyment. Avoid filling the workshop with mechanically similar variants without evidence.
