# Experiment 011 — Equipment versus mentorship spending

`node tools/spending-study.js` uses 30 mixed-investment one-hour snapshots and 30 four-hour snapshots. Replace each class with a freshly generated standard-technique member. Compare hire only, hire plus mentorship, hire plus a 120-Gold Ritual focus pattern equipped on the entire party, and both. Pay actual costs before quoting affordable catch-up. Test the next Gatekeeper at fixed current stats without intervening XP. All 720 bounded fights validate terminal saves. Desired same-class recruit access is assumed; applicant search costs are omitted.

| Session / class | Fresh wins | Mentored wins | Ritual party wins | Both wins |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 hour / Guardian | 0/30 | 29/30 | 26/30 | 30/30 |
| 1 hour / Ranger | 1/30 | 30/30 | 11/30 | 30/30 |
| 1 hour / Cleric | 1/30 | 30/30 | 25/30 | 30/30 |
| 4 hours / Guardian | 0/30 | 16/30 | 6/30 | 29/30 |
| 4 hours / Ranger | 5/30 | 12/30 | 6/30 | 17/30 |
| 4 hours / Cleric | 0/30 | 13/30 | 5/30 | 28/30 |

Mean total spending at one hour: fresh 271, mentorship 12,382.9, ritual 391, both 12,420.1 Gold. Four-hour spending: 648.7 / 54,825 / 768.7 / 54,867.4. Both strategies quote training after buying equipment, so their affordable training targets can differ.

Equipment can be a much cheaper first step for Guardian/Cleric replacement, but cannot consistently replace mentorship, especially for Rangers. Combined preparation solves more later fights while consuming substantial savings. Preserve both choices and current prices. These samples do not prove universal optimal shopping or enjoyment; more equipment arrangements and party compositions remain outside this comparison.
